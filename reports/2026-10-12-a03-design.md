# 2026-10-12 — A-03: spiking + learning for sequential tasks (design lane)

**Status: FROZEN as of 2026-10-12.** This is the A-03 *design-lane*
report: the design of the sequential-tasks experiment, what the pre-
registration assumed, what the measurements forced to change, and what
the pilot showed about the design. The 210-run main grid is
pre-registered (README, committed before any run) and is a scheduled
post-heavy-lane campaign; its numbers will land in a follow-up report
(`2026-10-XX-a03-main-grid.md`) and will supersede nothing here,
because this report makes no outcome claims beyond the pilot's sizing
read-out.

## Goal

A-01 showed the 1,536-neuron olfactory circuit (FlyWire FAFB v783) has
an advantage over shuffled wirings on *static* single-shot tasks once
its per-edge gains are trained; A-02 showed the advantage survives
learning under several shuffles and quantified it; B-01 ran the same
circuit as a spiking LIF network (Shiu et al. 2024) on a one-shot
compass task. A-03 is the combination neither covered: **does a
spiking network whose wiring is fixed, or a rate network whose wiring
learns, handle *sequential* tasks — tasks whose answer depends on a
sequence of inputs, not a single input — and what does the wiring
contribute once the task has a time axis?**

The design lane (this report) fixes the question into a runnable,
pre-registered experiment within the campaign's hard envelope
(≤2048 nodes, ≤20 epochs, no heavy runs while the B-02 lane is
active).

## Setup

**Circuit.** The same 1,536-neuron / 42,921-edge signed olfactory
atlas as A-01/B-01 (`sha256 ef6a6c1d…`, staged file, pinned in the
README), 256 sensory input nodes (food olfactory receptor neurons),
384 output nodes (mushroom-body output + lateral-horn appositions).

**Tasks** (`tasks.py`, deterministic, seed 20261012, 1536 trials each,
512-trial held-out fold disjoint from training):

| key | kind | T steps × d | question |
|-----|------|-------------|----------|
| T-del | 4-class | 8 × 4 | which of 4 tones appeared (delayed match: one tone in a 4-step middle window, identity must be held to the end) |
| T-ang | reg (sin,cos) | 10 × 2 | final heading after a sequence of signed rotations |
| T-xor | 2-class | 12 × 3 | XOR of two binary sub-streams, decided at the end |

Non-saturation check (pre-registered): the tangent-optimal linear
readout of the *input features* (the ceiling the trained models are
compared against) scores 0.907 (T-del), 45.5° RMCE (T-ang), 0.859
(T-xor) — none saturated, none at chance.

**Wiring controls** (`controls.py`, byte-identical A-01 copy,
`sha256`-verified): fly, degree, sign, weight, population, shuffled,
disconnected — same multiset of edge magnitudes (and, for degree/
population/sign, the same structural statistics), different
arrangements.

**Dynamics (arms)** (`model.py`):

- `tanh-*` (6 wirings): A-02's treatment verbatim — trainable per-edge
  gains (zero-init → 1.0), per-node leaks (→ 0.5), per-node biases (→
  0), encoder/drive/readout/head — with the one A-03 change: a
  *persistent* hidden state across the T input steps (BPTT), not
  A-02's per-decision re-initialization.
- `spike-*` (6 wirings): the B-01 LIF dynamics (Shiu 2024 `lif.py`,
  byte-identical, pinned by sha256) with the circuit **frozen** and a
  fixed declared Poisson drive to the 256 sensory nodes; the only
  learned object is a ridge readout on mean-spike-count features. Δw =
  0 by construction.
- `gru` / `mlp`: conventional comparators (1×GRU-128; MLP on a window
  of the last 4–5 steps — the declared no-long-memory comparator).

**One learner** (`learner.py`): Adam 2.5e-3, batch 64, ≤20 epochs,
early-stopping patience 5 on the internal 10% val split, checkpoint =
best val **loss** (the A-02 rule, unit-tested against the exact A-02
failure shape where best val *accuracy* would have checkpointed a
collapsed run), held-out fold touched exactly once, Δw recorded per
tanh run.

## Pre-registration assumptions vs. what measurement forced

The pre-registration (README, commit `189014d`, written before any run)
froze a spike drive mapping that turned out to be **dynamically
inert**: the per-target-normalized 0.275 mV efficacy on this signed
atlas produces max synaptic conductance 0.045 mV (below the 0.5 mV
spike threshold) with zero spikes, and the 4 mV readout tonic leaves
the 384 output nodes at v_ss = −48 mV, below the −45 mV threshold —
the readout population cannot fire at all. Both are *design* defects
of the frozen text, found by the pilot's own purpose (measure, don't
trust the projection). Declared corrections, recorded in the README
"Sizing outcome" so the frozen text stays the frozen text:

1. **Efficacy** = sign × min(log1p|w|, c) × k, with the per-edge effect
   p95-scaled to 4.7 mV — B-01's own capped-max *regime* (min(PSD,
   p95=17) × 0.275 mV), not its PSD formula (this circuit file is the
   signed atlas, A-01's).
2. **Tonic 10 mV** on the outputs → v_ss = −42 mV: a live,
   ~refractory-limited population; the ridge standardization removes
   the baseline. Verified live: 134,217 spikes in one 800 ms trial,
   351 of 384 outputs receiving synaptic input.
3. **Per-trial reset = fresh `LIFNet` construction.** The pinned B-01
   `lif.py` `reset()` is latent-broken (`fill_` called with a 1-d
   tensor on the multi-dimensional state fields; B-01 never called it
   — single-session network). A-03 never calls it; the constructor
   state *is* B-01's initial state, so the pinned file stays
   byte-identical. The defect is logged for B-02 Stage 1.
4. **tanh propagations 4 → 2 per input step** (A-02 used 4). One
   propagation on the 42,921-edge circuit costs ~95 ms at batch 64
   (48 ms edge scatter + 35 ms tanh/leak + 12 ms allocation); batch
   size does not amortize it. 4 propagations per step put the grid
   ~5× over the pre-registered wall projection; 2 settling
   propagations per 100 ms step is the minimal revision (T × 2
   propagations per trial is *more* total depth than A-02's
   4-per-decision, distributed over time).
5. **LIF loop single-threaded** (71 µs/step vs 820 µs at the
   campaign's 4 threads — the 1,536-element kernels are launch-bound);
   the tanh arm keeps 4 threads.
6. **Shrink-ladder step (2)** applied to the tanh arm: 256 → 128 train
   trials (held-out fold untouched).

## Pilot (sizing; seed 100, 32-trial held-out prefix, 4 epochs)

18/18 runs in 9 min total wall (results/pilot_*.json, committed).
Not the arbiter — the full grid is — but it answers the design
questions the lane owns:

| arm | T-del (acc; lin. ceiling .907) | T-ang (RMCE°; ceiling 45.5°) | T-xor (acc; ceiling .859) | wall s |
|-----|-------------------------------|------------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| tanh-fly | 0.281 | 87.4 | 0.488 | 7–9 |
| tanh-degree | 0.281 | 86.7 | 0.488 | 3–5 |
| spike-fly | **1.000** | **0.05** | 0.547 | 42–64 |
| spike-degree | **1.000** | **0.04** | 0.520 | 43–64 |
| gru | 0.250 (chance) | 86.6 (chance 89.8) | 0.488 | <1 |
| mlp | 0.250 (chance) | 96.7 | 0.863 | <1 |

Design read-out (no outcome claims beyond this):

- **The corrected spike mapping is learnable.** With the frozen
  mapping the arm was *undefined* (no spikes, no readout population);
  with the corrected one, a *frozen* spiking circuit with only a ridge
  readout saturates T-del and T-ang within a single training pass at
  32 trials. The spike arm is far ahead of its rate/tanh counterparts
  *at this training budget* — the arms have very different learning
  curves, which is exactly what the main grid's 20-epoch budget and
  the val-LOSS checkpoint rule exist to equalize before comparing.
- **The ceiling column is a linear reference, not an upper bound.**
  mlp T-xor (0.863) exceeds the 0.859 tangent-optimal *linear*
  ceiling: a nonlinear 5-window cascade is not bounded by it. Any
  follow-up comparison against ceilings must say so.
- **Cost model.** spike ≈ 1.3–2.0 s/trial (the 200 ms readout window
  costs 2 LIF substeps per report step), tanh (2-prop) ≈ 0.02 s per
  epoch-trial. Revised grid projection: ≈28 h of background wall for
  210 runs (spike-dominated), i.e. a scheduled post-heavy-lane
  campaign of ~1.2 days — superseding the pre-registered 10–15 h
  projection, which rested on the wrong "1–3 min per tanh run"
  estimate. The lane's hard rules (≤2048 nodes, ≤20 epochs, no heavy
  runs while B-02 is active) are met; every individual run is
  minutes.

## What failed

- The frozen spike mapping (per-target-normalized efficacy + 4 mV
  tonic): inert, as documented above. Root cause: the mapping was
  written from B-01's *unweighted synthetic* circuit PSD, not from
  this signed atlas's small-integer weights.
- `make_rate_model`/`TREATMENTS` key handling in the first driver
  draft (arm names vs. wiring names): fixed in `023f3ed`/`60a7f2a`
  before any official run.
- B-01's `reset()` latent bug: not fixed (pinned file stays
  byte-identical); worked around by construction; logged for B-02.

## What the numbers do not show

- Nothing about the *main* grid: wiring-effect sizes, seed variance,
  or the fly-vs-shuffle contrast at full training budget. The pilot
  is 1 seed × 32 held-out trials × 4 epochs; it sizes the design, it
  does not adjudicate the question.
- No claim that the spike arm's early saturation is wiring-specific:
  spike-fly and spike-degree both saturate T-del/T-ang identically at
  this budget — the interesting comparison is the main grid's.
- No GPU/cuda claim: everything here is CPU.

## Review note (2026-10-12 ~02:1x, coordinator)

The Goal paragraph (lines 1–3) misstates the frozen results it builds
on. Correct record: **A-01's** only non-null was the *frozen* spiking
stimulus-response regime (~3.7× over controls, first campaign); its
tanh-rate learning regime was null. **A-02** (the trainable-tanh
treatment) was a null: no criterion flipped vs A-01 (see
`2026-10-12-a02-learnable-gains.md` and its review note). So A-03 is
not a follow-up to a surviving advantage — it is the only corner of
the 2×2 (dynamics × learning) grid that had never been run, which is
exactly what this design addresses; nothing in this report's design or
pilot sizing depends on the misstatement. The frozen text above is
left as written; this note supersedes that sentence only.
